# Preferred Sources

This file is the search allowlist. It is not always-on. Read it when researching, teaching, or writing a lesson. Then search or fetch from these sites. Cite the specific post or paper. Do not invent citations.

Pick the list that matches the topic. Do not drag data-engineering blogs into a microstructure lesson, or trading-desk blogs into a Spark lesson, unless the idea actually belongs.

If a claim is not in one of these sources or another high-quality primary source (paper, exchange/FINRA/SEC doc, textbook), say so and do not guess.

## Markets and microstructure

Use this list for this repo's curriculum: participants, orders, liquidity, impact, indexes, ETFs, rebalances, and flows.

- [Jane Street Blog](https://blog.janestreet.com/)
- [Optiver Insights](https://optiver.com/insights/)
- Academic papers (SSRN, NBER, journal PDFs). Prefer the original paper over a recap.
- Exchange, FINRA, and SEC primary docs when the topic is a rule or a venue.

Also acceptable when they have a real desk write-up: DRW, Millennium, Renaissance, or a comparable trading-firm note. Still cite the page.

## Data engineering

Use this list only when the question is Spark, Databricks, pipelines, warehouses, or similar.

- [Start Data Engineering](https://www.startdataengineering.com/)
- [Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/blog/engineering/)
- [Seattle Data Guy](https://seattledataguy.substack.com/)
- [Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/)
- [Netflix TechBlog](https://netflixtechblog.com/)

Jane Street still belongs here when the topic is their engineering, not their trading.

## How to use these

1. Search the relevant site for the mechanism, not a slogan.
2. Open the post or paper and cite title plus URL.
3. Teach the mechanism. The blog is evidence, not the lesson structure.
4. If two sources disagree, say so.
